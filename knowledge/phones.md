# Buying a used phone

Battery health. On iPhones, open Settings, Battery, Battery Health. Below about 80 percent the battery is considered worn and a replacement is likely soon, so a low figure justifies a lower price. On Android, ask for a battery report or a screenshot of battery usage.

Activation lock and accounts. Make sure the seller signs out of their Apple ID or Google account and removes the screen lock before you pay. A phone still tied to an account can become unusable.

Screen and repairs. Look for cracks, dead touch zones and a screen replaced with a non-original part. Third-party screens are cheaper and often have weaker colours and brightness.

Water damage and body condition. Dents, bent frames and a swollen back cover can mean a drop or battery swelling. Ask whether the phone has ever been in water.

IMEI and SIM. Check that the IMEI on the box and in settings match, and that the phone works with your SIM. Ask for the original box, charger and receipt.

Storage and model. Confirm the storage size in settings, since a 128GB and a 256GB model can look identical. Price differences between storage sizes can be large.

Warranty. Ask whether any manufacturer or store warranty remains, and ask for the proof of purchase.
