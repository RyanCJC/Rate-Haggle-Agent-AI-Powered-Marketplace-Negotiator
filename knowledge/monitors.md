# Buying a used monitor

Dead pixels and stuck pixels. Ask the seller to show a plain white screen and then a plain black screen. Dead pixels show as tiny dots that never change colour. Do this in person before paying.

Backlight bleed and uneven brightness. On a black screen in a dim room, bright patches near the edges mean backlight bleed. A little is normal, a lot is a reason to lower your offer.

Panel type. IPS panels usually have better colours and viewing angles. TN panels are cheaper and often faster but look washed out from the side. VA panels have deep blacks but can smear in fast motion.

Refresh rate. A 60Hz monitor is fine for office work. For gaming, 144Hz or higher is a real upgrade, so a listing that claims 144Hz should be checked in the display settings.

Ports and cables. Check that the ports match your computer: HDMI, DisplayPort or USB-C. Missing original cables are common and reduce the fair price.

Stand and mount. A wobbly stand or missing VESA screws are small faults. Ask whether the stand is included.

Age and brightness. Older monitors may have dimmer backlights. Ask how many years it has been used and how many hours a day.

Warranty and receipt. A receipt with remaining warranty adds value. Without one, assume no warranty and negotiate accordingly.
