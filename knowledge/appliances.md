# Buying a used home appliance

Power it on. Ask to see it running before you pay. Check that it starts, makes its normal sounds and does its main job, such as cooling, washing, heating or cleaning.

Age and model. Ask for the brand, model number and year. Older models can be less efficient and may have no spare parts. Check that the plug and voltage suit your home.

Fridges and freezers. Check that it gets cold, which takes time, so ask how long it has been running. Look at the door seals for cracks or gaps, check for heavy frost and for bad smells. A fridge that was laid on its side should stand upright for a few hours before it is switched on.

Air conditioners. Ask for the compressor's condition, the age and when it was last serviced. Check that it cools, does not leak water and does not rattle. Remember that moving and installing a unit usually costs extra, so ask for that cost before agreeing a price.

Washing machines and dryers. Run a short cycle if you can. Look for leaks, a drum that wobbles or makes a grinding sound, and a drain pump that works. Check that the door seal is intact.

Small appliances. For microwaves, ovens, air fryers and rice cookers, check that they heat properly, that the door and latch work, and that the cord and plug are undamaged.

Safety. Do not buy an appliance with a damaged cord, burn marks, a strong electrical smell or signs of water inside.

Warranty and service. Ask for the receipt, any remaining warranty and the service history. Without a warranty, assume you carry the repair risk.
