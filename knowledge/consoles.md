# Buying a used game console

Accounts and locks. Make sure the console is signed out of the seller's account and has no lock or ban. Ask the seller to reset it to factory settings in front of you. A console still tied to someone else's account can be taken back or become unusable.

Modified consoles. Ask whether the console has been modified to run copied games. Modified consoles can be banned from online play, and the ban stays with the console.

Controller drift. Check every controller for stick drift: leave the sticks untouched and watch whether the cursor or camera moves by itself. Test all buttons, triggers and the charging port. Drift is a common fault and costs money to repair.

Storage. Ask how much storage the console has and how much is free. Internal storage is hard to upgrade on some models, so a larger size is worth more.

Heat and noise. Play a game for ten minutes. A very loud fan or sudden shutdowns point to dust, old thermal paste or a failing part.

Disc drive and ports. If the console has a disc drive, test it with a real disc. Check the HDMI port, USB ports and power connector for loose or bent pins.

Cables and extras. Ask whether the power cable, video cable, controllers and any bundled games are included. Missing items add to the real cost.

Warranty. A receipt with remaining warranty adds value. Without one, assume none and keep some money aside for repairs.
