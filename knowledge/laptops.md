# Buying a used laptop

Battery cycles and capacity. Ask for a battery report. A high cycle count or low full charge capacity means short battery life and a possible replacement cost.

Storage and memory. Confirm the RAM and the SSD size in the system settings. Check the drive health with a free tool before paying, since a failing drive is expensive to fix.

Screen and keyboard. Look for dead pixels, flickering, backlight bleed and worn or missing keys. Test every key and the trackpad.

Hinges and body. Open and close the lid several times. A loose or cracked hinge is a common fault on older laptops.

Heat and noise. Run the laptop under load for a few minutes. A loud fan or very high temperature may mean dried thermal paste or dust, and it can reduce the price.

Ports and charging. Test every port, the charger and the headphone jack. A missing original charger is a reason to negotiate.

Software locks. Make sure the laptop is not tied to a work account, an Apple ID or a BIOS password. Ask the seller to sign out and reset it.

Warranty and receipt. Ask for the receipt and any remaining warranty, and check the serial number against the listing.
