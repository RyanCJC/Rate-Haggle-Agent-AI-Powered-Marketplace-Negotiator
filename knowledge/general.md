# Buying any second-hand item

Test it before you pay. Ask to see the item working, in person if you can, or in a short video that shows it switched on. Never pay a deposit for something nobody has shown you working.

Reason for selling. Ask why it is being sold. A clear, ordinary reason such as upgrading, moving or not using it is reassuring. A vague or changing story is a reason to slow down.

Condition and wear. Look at the places that wear first: corners, hinges, buttons, handles, edges and anything that moves. Ask for close-up photos of scratches, dents and stains instead of relying on "good condition".

Missing parts. Ask what is included: original box, cables, chargers, remotes, manuals, keys and spare parts. Missing parts lower the fair price, and some are expensive or impossible to replace.

Proof of purchase. A receipt, invoice or warranty card shows the item is genuine and may carry remaining warranty. Without one, assume there is no warranty and negotiate accordingly.

Price against new. Check what the same item costs new and what similar used ones are listed at. A used price close to the new price needs a good reason. A price far below the market needs an explanation.

Honest disclosure. Ask directly about repairs, drops, water damage and known faults. A seller who answers plainly is easier to deal with than one who dodges.

Meetup and payment. Meet in a public place, bring a friend for valuable items, check the item before paying and pay only when you are happy. Do not accept a rushed decision because another buyer is supposedly coming.
