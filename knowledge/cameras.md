# Buying a used camera or lens

Shutter count. The shutter wears out after many photos. Ask for the shutter count, which many cameras can show through free software on a computer, and compare it with the rated life for that model. A low count on an old camera is better than a high one.

Sensor. Dust on the sensor is common and can be cleaned. Scratches or stuck pixels are not. Take a photo of a plain bright wall at a small aperture and look for dark spots.

Lens condition. Shine a torch through the lens. Fungus looks like thin web patterns, haze looks like a milky film, and both are expensive to fix. Small dust inside is normal. Check the front and rear glass for scratches.

Focus and zoom. Test autofocus in good light and in dim light. Listen for grinding noises. Turn the zoom and focus rings and check they move smoothly without play.

Body and mount. Look for dents, loose grips, worn rubber and a wobbly lens mount. Signs of a fall or of water, such as corrosion in the battery compartment, are a reason to walk away.

Screen and viewfinder. Check the rear screen and the viewfinder for dead pixels, burn marks and scratches. Test every button and dial.

Battery and card slot. Ask how many batteries come with it and how long they last. Insert a memory card, take a few photos and play them back to confirm the slot works.

Accessories and warranty. Ask about the charger, strap, caps, original box and receipt. A receipt with remaining warranty adds value. Otherwise assume none.
