# Buying used furniture

Measure first. Measure the space where it will go and also the doorways, stairs, lift and corners it has to pass through. Many bargains fail because they do not fit through the door.

Structure. Push and rock the piece. A wobble, creaking joints or loose legs may be fixable with tightening but can also mean damaged frames. Look underneath and at the back, where problems are hidden.

Material. Solid wood lasts and can be repaired. Particleboard and thin veneer swell when wet and do not survive moving well. Ask what the piece is made of.

Surface. Look for water rings, deep scratches, peeling veneer and sun fading. Small marks lower the price a little, but warping and peeling are bigger faults.

Upholstery and mattresses. Check fabric for stains, tears, smells and pet hair. For mattresses and sofas, look at seams and corners for signs of bed bugs: tiny dark spots and shed skins. If you see any, do not buy.

Drawers, doors and hinges. Open and close every drawer and door. Check runners, handles and hinges for wear, and make sure the keys and locks work.

Hardware and assembly. Ask whether it can be taken apart for transport and whether all screws, brackets and fittings are included. Missing hardware can be hard to match.

Pickup. Large items need a vehicle and often two people. Agree who carries it and who pays for delivery before you commit.
